Does ServiceTitan Track Certificate-of-Insurance Expiration Dates for You?

Top Builder AI Published August 1, 2026 ~11 min read HVAC · Plumbing · Electrical · Roofing Updated August 1, 2026

The 38-second version of the gap below, straight from ServiceTitan's own Forms, e-signature, and vendor-contract documentation.

Not on its own. By ServiceTitan's own published documentation, Forms collects checklists, photos, PDFs and customer signatures, and e-signature is scoped to service agreements sent to customers. Neither documented workflow classifies an incoming document, reads an expiration date off it, or alerts anyone before a certificate of insurance lapses. Here is the part that makes the gap impossible to wave away: ServiceTitan's own vendor contract requires exactly that discipline from the businesses it works with — a certificate of insurance within ten days of signing, thirty days' written notice before any cancellation. ServiceTitan enforces this on its own vendors. It does not hand contractors the equivalent tool to enforce it on theirs.

This is not a knock on Forms or e-signature. Both do a real job well: capturing what happened on a job site and getting a customer's name on a service agreement without a clipboard. The question this post answers is narrower — what happens to the paperwork that isn't a service agreement: a subcontractor's certificate of insurance, a signed lien waiver, a permit, a manufacturer warranty registration — and who catches it when a certificate quietly expires.

What does ServiceTitan's Forms feature actually track?

Per ServiceTitan's own Forms documentation, the feature is built for field data collection: technicians fill out checklists, attach photos, generate PDFs, and capture customer signatures on service agreements during a job. That is genuinely useful for standardizing what a tech documents on site.

What the documentation does not describe is any classification layer sitting behind that capture. A completed form is a completed form. Nothing in ServiceTitan's own published workflow reads what type of document arrived, extracts a date buried inside it, or routes it to a specific department or folder based on what it actually is.

Does ServiceTitan's e-signature feature cover certificates of insurance?

No. ServiceTitan's own how-to guide scopes e-signature specifically to service agreements: you add a signature link, email it to a customer, and get notified "when a customer views or signs an agreement." That is the entire documented alert surface — a customer-facing signature event on one document type. There is no vendor or subcontractor document category in that workflow, and no mention of expiration-date alerting for anything.

If ServiceTitan doesn't track COI expiration, why does ServiceTitan itself require it?

Because when ServiceTitan is the one holding the risk, it treats certificate-of-insurance compliance as non-negotiable. ServiceTitan's own Standard Vendor Terms and Conditions require any vendor it contracts with to furnish a certificate of insurance within ten (10) days of signing the agreement, naming ServiceTitan as additional insured, with at least thirty (30) days' prior written notice before any coverage cancellation.

Read that against the product ServiceTitan sells contractors. ServiceTitan requires proof-of-insurance discipline, with real deadlines and real notice periods, from the businesses that work with it. It does not build the tracking, matching, and expiration-alerting layer that would let a contractor enforce the same discipline on its own subcontractors, inside the same system. The company knows this risk is worth a contractual deadline. The product it ships doesn't watch the calendar for you.

What does a lapsed COI actually cost a contractor?

There is no single authoritative number here, and this post will not pretend there is. A couple of compliance-software vendors that publish content on COI tracking put the cost of a claim involving an uninsured subcontractor somewhere in the $75,000 to $150,000 range, citing unspecified "construction industry data." We could not independently verify a primary source behind that figure, so treat it as directional, not a guarantee for any specific job.

What is verifiable is the mechanism, not the dollar figure: a general contractor, a bonding company, or ServiceTitan itself can require current proof of insurance before releasing payment or continuing a relationship. A certificate that lapsed three weeks ago and nobody noticed is exactly what triggers that hold — on a job that is otherwise finished and waiting to be paid.

What do existing "AI document management for contractors" tools actually offer?

Two kinds of pages currently answer some version of this question, and neither closes the actual gap. Generic AI-document-management content describes, in purely educational terms, an AI system that can "ingest COIs, extract policy limits, and flag deficiencies" — useful as a concept, but it names no platform, offers no buyer-decision framing, and never mentions ServiceTitan or field service at all. On the other side, dedicated certificate-of-insurance trackers exist as standalone compliance software: real expiration reminders, but built to run independently, with no documented integration to a contractor's job data, customer records, or ServiceTitan folder structure.
