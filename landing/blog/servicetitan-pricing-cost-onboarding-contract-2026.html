How Much Does ServiceTitan Actually Cost in 2026?

Top Builder AI Published August 16, 2026 Last updated: August 16, 2026 ~13 min read HVAC · Plumbing · Electrical

The 47-second version: what ServiceTitan's own site, and its own Terms of Use, actually say about cost.

ServiceTitan does not publish a price anywhere, confirmed directly on its own pricing page, on Capterra, and on G2, so there is no official number to report. What does exist is a real paper trail: ServiceTitan's own Terms of Use spell out how its contracts and termination fees work, and its own BBB complaints profile contains specific, dated, named dollar figures real contractors say they actually paid, a $4,802.50 onboarding fee, a $2,303 monthly bill, a $120,000 annual bill. This piece works from those documents directly, not from a guessed average, and labels clearly which numbers are confirmed and which are third-party estimates.

A companion piece on this site already covers what it costs to leave ServiceTitan once you're locked in, migration effort, retraining, and the same BBB termination-fee figures used there. This piece is narrower and comes first in the buying sequence: what does ServiceTitan cost to get into in the first place, what's the onboarding fee, and what do you actually sign.

How much does ServiceTitan cost per month?

There is no public monthly price to quote, and that absence is itself the finding. ServiceTitan's own pricing page, read directly, shows three tiers, Starter, Essentials, and The Works, each with a "Request Pricing" button instead of a dollar figure, and states "our per-technician pricing is designed to fit your business and goals, at any size" without ever stating what that pricing is.

What real contractors have actually reported paying, straight from ServiceTitan's own BBB complaints profile: Tiger Air's complaint (filed April 2026) cites a $2,303 monthly bill; a separate complaint (filed September 2025) cites $1,400 a month "across all contacts and locations"; and Bold City's complaint (filed May 2026) states $120,000 a year paid to ServiceTitan. None of those three complaints specify technician count, so they cannot be converted into a clean per-tech rate, but they are real, dated, named figures, not estimates.

Why doesn't ServiceTitan publish its pricing anywhere?

Because its entire pricing motion is built around a sales conversation, not a rate card, and that's consistent everywhere the pricing question comes up. Capterra's ServiceTitan pricing page, read directly, lists the starting price as "Not available" and shows all three plans as "Custom Quote." Searching for the same information on G2 turns up no published figure either. Three independent sources, ServiceTitan's own site plus the two largest software-review marketplaces, all land on the same answer: a demo is the only way to get a number.

That opacity is also the actual driver behind most "switch to a competitor" conversations, covered in more depth in the companion piece on whether to replace ServiceTitan or add an AI layer on top of it. It rarely shows up as one clean complaint about the software itself; it shows up as an owner who can't get a straight number before signing.

How much is ServiceTitan's onboarding or implementation fee?

ServiceTitan's own onboarding page describes the process, not the price. ServiceTitan's own onboarding feature page, read directly, states the process "ensures that your entire team is efficiently trained" and is "designed to maximize your time learning ServiceTitan without any major disruptions to business," with no fee or timeline disclosed anywhere on the page.

The one number that is confirmed comes from an actual signed contract, not marketing copy: Quality Comfort's BBB complaint (filed April 2026) states the shop paid $4,802.50 via ACH for onboarding and initial fees as part of a 24-month, $96,000 total contract, before a later dispute over a $67,230 early-termination demand once the implementation reportedly never reached production-ready status. That is a real number, from a real filed complaint, tied to a real contract value.

What are ServiceTitan's contract terms, and what happens if you cancel early?

Per ServiceTitan's own Terms of Use, the default is month-to-month, but that default rarely governs what a shop actually signs. ServiceTitan's own Terms of Use state that if the Terms of Use are the only agreement in place, subscriptions "are month-to-month and will automatically renew for additional successive monthly terms unless either party provides not less than thirty (30) days written notice of nonrenewal." In practice, contractors sign a separate order form with a specified term, and that's where the real exposure sits: the same Terms of Use state plainly, "If you want to terminate the Agreement before the term of the Agreement is over, you will owe a termination fee equal to all the remaining payments," and separately that "all fees are in U.S. Dollars and are non-refundable."

Real contracts visible through BBB complaints back this up directly: Quality Comfort signed a 24-month term; a separate 2026 complaint on this site's companion piece documents a 2-year term. A Software Advice reviewer, writing independently of any complaint, described the same structure in plain language: "If you wish to cancel you'll have to pay the remaining roughly 22k balance to get out," and separately noted a narrow 30-day cancellation window after the initial term before the contract locks into another renewal cycle.

Do the "Pro" add-ons cost extra on top of the base subscription?

Yes, and ServiceTitan's own site confirms the structure even without confirming the price. ServiceTitan's own Pricebook Pro feature page, read directly, lists Pricebook Pro under a "Get more with Pro" upgrade section alongside Marketing Pro, Fleet Pro, and the AI Virtual Agent, positioning all of them as optional add-ons layered on top of the base subscription. No pricing is shown on that page for any of them.

Reviewers describe the effect of that structure in plain terms. On Capterra, reviewers cite "costly add-ons, undisclosed fees, and a pricing structure that challenges smaller businesses" as a specific criticism, distinct from praise for the platform's core feature depth. A separate 2026 companion piece on this site, covering whether ServiceTitan fits a smaller trades shop, notes the same base-plus-add-on structure from the buyer's side: the sticker shock rarely comes from one number, it comes from stacking several unpriced modules on top of a base subscription that also wasn't priced up front.

How much should a shop realistically budget for ServiceTitan in year one?

Enough to justify getting a real quote before signing anything, because no single public number covers every variable that goes into what a shop actually pays. What's genuinely confirmed: a per-technician subscription model with no published rate, an onboarding fee that was $4,802.50 in the one documented case above, contract terms that run 24 months or longer in the real, signed agreements visible through BBB filings, a termination clause that ServiceTitan's own Terms of Use state equals "all the remaining payments," and Pro-tier add-ons priced separately from the base plan.

What's genuinely not confirmed: any single "ServiceTitan costs $X/tech/month" figure repeated across the dozens of contractor buying-guide sites that all converge on a similar $245-$500/tech/month range without citing a common, checkable source. That range is worth knowing as a directional planning number, not worth treating as ServiceTitan's actual rate card, because ServiceTitan has never published one.
