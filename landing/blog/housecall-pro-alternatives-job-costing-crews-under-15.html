6 Housecall Pro Alternatives for Contractors Who Need Job Costing Under 15 Techs

Top Builder AI Published August 24, 2026 Updated September 1, 2026 ~11 min read Software Comparison

Housecall Pro's own pricing page already claims job costing, "track and report the costs associated with each job," on its cheapest plan, $59/month. So "does Housecall Pro have job costing" is the wrong question. The real one, the one that actually changes what a shop pays: which tier, on which tool, turns that checkbox into a number an owner trusts enough to act on. We checked six tools' own current pricing pages to answer it, not a roundup that repeats last year's screenshot.

None of this is a knock on Housecall Pro specifically. It's a genuinely good scheduling-and-dispatch tool for a huge swath of home-service trades. The pattern below shows up because "job costing" means something different, and costs something different, on every platform a growing shop compares it against, and almost nobody lines up all six pricing pages side by side before picking.

Jobber

Jobber's own pricing page reserves job costing for its Grow plan and above. Core ($21/month, 1 user) and Connect ($70/month, 5 users included) don't have it; Grow ($105/month, 10 users included) adds "track profitability with job costing," and Plus ($280/month, 15 users included) adds automated job costing and profitability tracking. GPS tracking, notably, ships on every Jobber tier including Core, not gated behind Grow the way job costing is. Extra users run $29/month each on any plan.

For a shop of 6-10 people specifically evaluating Housecall Pro's Essentials plan ($149/month, 5 users included, no job costing named at that tier), Jobber's Grow plan lands close on price with twice the included seats and a named job-costing feature Housecall Pro doesn't call out at the equivalent tier.

Service Fusion

Service Fusion prices job costing as a paid add-on at its entry tier, not a Plus-and-up feature the way Jobber does it. Starter is $208/month (annual billing, unlimited users included) with QuickBooks Online and Desktop sync built in, and job costing available as an add-on; Plus, at $325/month, makes job costing a standard included feature alongside inventory management; Pro, at $533/month, keeps everything from Plus.

The unlimited-user model is the real differentiator here versus Jobber's per-seat pricing: a 15-tech crew pays the same $208-533/month band a 3-tech crew does, which changes the math for shops that expect to add headcount without a per-seat bill climbing alongside it.

Workiz

Workiz doesn't publish a monthly price for any of its three plans, Standard, Pro, and Ultimate, as of this writing; its pricing page routes every plan to a "request pricing" call. QuickBooks Online sync is listed as a standard feature across all three tiers. Job costing is not named as a feature on Workiz's own pricing page at any tier, which doesn't mean the platform can't do cost tracking through a workaround, but it does mean a shop can't verify it, or its price, without a sales conversation.

FieldPulse

FieldPulse is seat-based and, like Workiz, doesn't list a monthly number publicly; its own pricing page routes Essentials, Professional, and Enterprise to a custom quote. QuickBooks sync is standard across all three tiers. Job costing, again, isn't named as a line-item feature on the pricing page itself, though third-party listings note broader accounting integrations (Zapier, Xero) alongside QuickBooks.

ServiceTitan

ServiceTitan is the tool most of these platforms get compared against once a shop is past the "which $100-300/month tool" conversation entirely, enterprise-grade job costing, dispatch, and reporting, at enterprise-grade implementation time and contract terms. We've covered the real onboarding timeline and contract structure in a dedicated pricing breakdown rather than re-deriving it here; the short version is that it's the right tool for a shop that has outgrown the "under 15 techs" framing this post is written for.

We started from the tools independent field-service comparison sites (Jobber's own competitor page, G2, Capterra) most consistently name as Housecall Pro's direct competitive set for crews under roughly 15 people, Jobber, Workiz, Service Fusion, and FieldPulse, then added the two ends of the spectrum a growing shop eventually lands on: ServiceTitan for the enterprise tier, and Top Builder AI as the tool built specifically around the reconciliation gap the other five leave to a bookkeeper. What to actually check before switching off Housecall Pro

Job costing existing on a pricing page and job costing being trustworthy enough to act on same-week are two different bars. Before paying more for a "job costing included" tier anywhere on this list, confirm what the feature actually reconciles against, your QuickBooks labor and material costs, not just the invoice total the field app already shows you.

1
Pull your current Housecall Pro job-cost report for your three most recent multi-day jobs and check it against what QuickBooks actually shows for labor and material cost on those same jobs.
2
If the two don't match, ask why before switching tools, a pricing-page feature won't fix a sync gap that a different platform will just reproduce.
3
Price the tier that includes job costing, not the entry tier, on whichever alternative you're evaluating; several of the tools above make that a real price jump, not a checkbox toggle.
4
Ask who reviews the number before it's trusted, a job-costing report nobody reconciles monthly is the same blind spot whether it lives in Housecall Pro, Jobber, or Service Fusion.

What else do contractors ask about Housecall Pro alternatives?

Does Housecall Pro have job costing?
Yes, at every published tier. Housecall Pro's own pricing page lists "track and report the costs associated with each job" starting on the $59/month (annual) Basic plan, one user included. The gap contractors actually hit isn't whether the feature exists, it's how granular that cost tracking gets as a crew grows past one truck.
Which Housecall Pro alternative has job costing on its cheapest plan?
None of the four direct competitors compared here do. Jobber reserves job costing for its $105/month Grow plan and up (Core and Connect, its two cheaper tiers, do not include it, per Jobber's own pricing page). Service Fusion sells job costing as a paid add-on on its $208/month Starter plan and only includes it standard starting at the $325/month Plus plan, per Service Fusion's own pricing page.
What does Jobber's Grow plan cost and what does it add over Housecall Pro?
$105/month billed annually, for 10 included users, per Jobber's own pricing page, which lists "track profitability with job costing" as a Grow-plan feature alongside GPS tracking (GPS is included on every Jobber tier, not just Grow). At 10 users, that's roughly double Housecall Pro's Essentials plan seat count ($149/month, 5 users included) for a plan that starts at a lower headline price.
Is Workiz or FieldPulse pricing public?
No. Both vendors' own pricing pages require a sales call or a "custom quote" request rather than listing a monthly number, as of this writing. Workiz's plans are named Standard, Pro, and Ultimate; FieldPulse's are Essentials, Professional, and Enterprise (seat-based). Both list QuickBooks Online sync as a standard feature across every tier; neither vendor's pricing page mentions job costing as a named feature.
Does Service Fusion's job costing cost extra?
At the entry tier, yes. Per Service Fusion's own pricing page, job costing is an optional add-on on the $208/month (annual) Starter plan, unlimited users included, and becomes a standard included feature starting at the $325/month Plus plan. QuickBooks Online and Desktop sync are included on every Service Fusion tier, including Starter.
How is Top Builder AI different from Jobber, Service Fusion, or ServiceTitan?
Job costing isn't a feature bolted onto a scheduling tool, it's a dedicated Financial agent that reconciles ServiceTitan and QuickBooks data into a per-job margin the operator reviews and approves, not a report a bookkeeper has to build by hand at month-end. There's no self-serve monthly tier to compare against a $105 or $208 line item; it's scoped per shop through a Teardown or Install engagement, per our own pricing page.
Do any of these tools sync with QuickBooks Online?
All five do, per each vendor's own pricing page: Housecall Pro (Essentials plan and up), Jobber, Workiz, Service Fusion (both QuickBooks Online and Desktop), and FieldPulse. QuickBooks sync being universal is exactly why job costing (not accounting sync) is the real differentiator between these tools.
What's the honest reason to leave Housecall Pro for a more expensive tool?
Granularity, not the presence of a job-costing checkbox. Housecall Pro's Basic plan already claims job-cost tracking at $59/month. Shops moving to a $105-plus Jobber Grow plan, a $325 Service Fusion Plus plan, or a scoped Top Builder AI engagement are almost always chasing per-technician labor burden, multi-day project cost rollups, or a margin number they trust enough to act on same-week, not the feature's existence.
