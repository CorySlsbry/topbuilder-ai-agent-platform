How to Automate Invoice Collections for Your ServiceTitan Shop: The A/R Aging Playbook (2026)

Top Builder AI Published June 24, 2026 ~13 min read HVAC · Plumbing · Electrical

To automate invoice collections on ServiceTitan, connect an AI agent to your open-invoice report, cross-reference it against QuickBooks to strip out unapplied-payment noise, score each remaining open invoice by age, balance, customer history, and job margin, then generate a ranked contact queue, text-payment-link for residential, call prompt for commercial, escalation flag for 90-plus, that an office person approves before any outreach reaches a customer. Top Builder AI's Financial agent runs this loop on a configurable cadence: it reconciles the two systems, ranks what to chase first and why, proposes the specific action for each invoice, and logs every approved contact back to ServiceTitan. Nothing reaches a customer without a human decision.

You can have a full dispatch board and a strong gross margin on paper and still run out of operating cash because the invoices you billed six weeks ago are still sitting in a pending column nobody is getting to. A ServiceTitan shop doing $700,000 a month in billings at a 45-day average collection cycle is carrying over $1 million in open receivables at any given point. That is not an accounting problem, it is a cash-flow problem, and it has a specific cause: the aging list exists, but a prioritized, ready-to-act queue does not.

This is not a fringe problem in the trades. Slow payments cost the U.S. construction sector a staggering $280 billion in 2024, and delayed payments increased costs by 14% of total construction spending that year, according to the Rabbet 2024 Construction Payments Report (2024). When cash sits in the A/R column instead of the bank, it is eroding margin on every dollar of work the shop does.

ServiceTitan's 2026 "AI in the Trades" report found that 74% of residential contractors see AI as key to operational efficiency. A/R collection is one of the areas where the gap between what the platform can do natively and what a purpose-built agent can do is widest, because the bottleneck is not billing, it is prioritized follow-up across dozens of open invoices while the office team is also running dispatch, scheduling, and customer service.

Why do contractors leave so much money sitting in the A/R column?

The mechanism is consistent across shops of every size. An invoice is generated in ServiceTitan when the technician closes the job. It gets emailed or texted to the customer. Some pay immediately, especially residential customers who pay by card in the field. Many do not. The residential customers who got a paper invoice forget. The commercial accounts have their own payment cycles, and $3,000 owed to a subcontractor does not always rise to the top of their AP queue by the 30-day mark.

The office team knows this. They have the aging report. What they do not have is a prioritized queue that tells them which of the 40 open invoices to call today, in what order, and what to say to each one. So the work defaults to oldest-first, or biggest-first, or whoever-happens-to-be-at-the-front-desk-first. Systematic follow-up, the kind that actually reduces Days Sales Outstanding, requires a scoring model the team does not have time to build manually every morning.

The trend is getting worse, not better. Across the industry, 82% of contractors now report payment delays of over 30 days, up from only 49% two years prior, per the Rabbet 2024 Construction Payments Report (2024). A separate survey of 250 U.S. general contractors and subcontractors found that 70% regularly face delayed payments, and that contractors inflate bids by an average of 8% to protect themselves against slow payment, according to Built / Talker Research (2025). The slow-pay problem is quietly inflating the price of every job in the market.

A second force multiplies the problem: the data itself is unreliable. The ServiceTitan aging report and the QuickBooks accounts receivable balance often do not match, because payments flow through both systems on different schedules and sometimes do not reconcile cleanly. Before you can prioritize the chase, you have to trust the list. Most shops do not yet have a process that guarantees that trust automatically.

Does ServiceTitan's native A/R module handle collections, and where does it leave gaps?

ServiceTitan has solid invoicing and collections infrastructure. Technicians can email or text invoices from the truck. Customers can pay online through the invoice link. The ServiceTitan Contractor Playbook on Collections documents the native AR workflow in detail. The aging report shows open balances by bucket: 0 to 30 days, 31 to 60, 61 to 90, and 90 and over. Bulk statement printing and emailing exist for volume follow-up. For many shops this is sufficient infrastructure for the problem they have.

Where it leaves a gap is prioritization and reconciliation. The aging report is a list, not a ranked queue. Every invoice in the 31-to-60 bucket looks the same, whether it is a $300 residential tune-up or a $14,000 commercial installation with a relationship that took three years to build. ServiceTitan does not know which one to call first, what to say, or whether the customer had a callback issue that should be resolved before any outreach goes out.

The platform also does not reconcile against QuickBooks automatically in real time. The two-way sync moves revenue and invoice status between systems, but it runs on a batch schedule. Between runs, a payment deposited in QuickBooks may not yet have cleared the corresponding invoice in ServiceTitan. The aging report in ServiceTitan may show an invoice as open when the money has already landed in the bank account.

What makes an invoice go stale between ServiceTitan and QuickBooks?

1
Unapplied payments A check or ACH arrives and is deposited in QuickBooks, but it is not linked to the specific invoice in ServiceTitan. ServiceTitan still shows the invoice open; QuickBooks shows the deposit without an invoice reference.
2
Credits and adjustments A service credit or adjustment is issued in one system but not mirrored in the other, creating a phantom balance that makes an invoice appear larger or smaller than it actually is.
3
Multi-invoice commercial customers A single check from a commercial account covers three invoices from the same month. The check is applied in QuickBooks to a general AR balance; the individual invoice links take extra steps in ServiceTitan that often get skipped during a busy week.

Chasing a reconciliation mismatch means calling a customer who already paid. That call costs goodwill. If it happens twice with the same account, it costs the relationship. The reconciliation has to precede the collection, and right now that reconciliation is a manual step that most shops skip or do quarterly at best.

Why don't ServiceTitan and QuickBooks A/R match, and why does it matter?

The practical question an office manager has to answer before making any collections call is: does this customer actually owe this money right now? The answer lives in two systems that neither system reconciles on its own in real time.

ServiceTitan knows the job, the invoice, and any payments received through its own payment processing. QuickBooks knows the bank deposits, the applied payments, the journal entries, and the credit memos. Where these two ledgers agree, you have a clean balance. Where they diverge, you have a reconciliation task disguised as a collections task.

The Financial agent resolves this by pulling both ledgers at the start of every cycle, matching customers and invoice references across the two systems, and flagging any invoice where the systems disagree. Those flagged invoices come out of the chase queue entirely and land in a separate cleanup list for the bookkeeper. What remains is the genuine outstanding balance, the invoices where both systems agree the money has not arrived.

That cleanup step alone, before any outreach is proposed, is often the most valuable output of the first cycle. Many shops find that 10 to 20 percent of their apparent 60-plus aging is actually reconciliation noise, not delinquency. Fixing that noise does not collect any cash directly, but it stops the office from burning goodwill on customers who have already paid.

The manual version of this work is expensive in staff hours. In broader business research, 65% of mid-sized businesses said they spend 14 hours per week on average on the administrative tasks tied to collecting payments, and those firms reported being owed $304,066 on average in late customer payments, according to QuickBooks / Intuit late-payments research (2021). That is the labor a prioritized, reconciled contact queue is designed to compress.

How does the AI Financial agent actually run a collections cycle?

After setup, the Financial agent runs on a configurable cadence: daily for high-volume shops, every two to three days for smaller operations. Each cycle has four steps.

1
Ingest the open-invoice report from ServiceTitan Invoice number, balance, customer, job type, technician, invoice date, last payment event, and any associated callback tasks or open dispute notes.
2
Cross-reference against QuickBooks AR Pull the QBO accounts receivable balance for the same customer set. Flag reconciliation mismatches, invoices where ST shows open and QBO shows a settled deposit, and route them to the bookkeeper queue. Remove them from the collections queue.
3
Score and rank the genuine open invoices Weight each by age, balance, customer payment history, and job margin. The output is a ranked list with a recommended first-touch action type: payment-link SMS, call prompt with brief, or escalation flag above threshold.
4
Propose the contact queue for human approval Each proposed action shows the customer, the balance, the recommended approach, and the reason. The office manager approves, edits, or skips each one. Approved actions log back to ServiceTitan. Nothing sends without a human decision.

How does the agent decide which invoices to chase first?

The aging report treats every invoice in the same bucket as equal priority. The scoring model does not. It weights four factors together to produce a ranked queue, not an alphabetical or oldest-first list.

Age. Older invoices weight more heavily because the probability of full collection decreases with time. An invoice at 75 days has a different risk profile from one at 38 days, even if the balance is identical. The weighting is not linear; the risk acceleration is steeper past 60 days.

Balance. Larger balances represent more gross profit dollars in the uncollected pile. A $9,200 commercial invoice at 42 days outranks a $280 residential tune-up at 58 days for the same daily contact budget. The model does not ignore small balances, but it allocates priority proportionally.

Customer payment history. A customer with a pattern of paying promptly after the first reminder needs a different first touch than one with a history of requiring two or three contacts. The model uses the logged outcomes of prior cycles to refine this dimension per customer over time. New customers with no history start at a moderate-priority default.

Job margin. The Financial agent knows the margin estimate on each job from the ServiceTitan job costing data. A high-margin job uncollected is a larger net loss than a thin-margin job at the same dollar amount. The model reflects that a $6,000 install at 58% gross margin is a bigger financial exposure than an $8,000 installation job at 18% margin.

The combined score produces a ranking that the office manager sees as a queue, not as a raw report. The top of the queue is the invoice where the combination of age, balance, history, and margin says "contact today, here is how."

What will the agent never do without my approval?

A few properties of the Financial agent are non-negotiable, regardless of shop size or volume:

  • No automated outreach. Every proposed contact requires a human to approve it first. The agent has no visibility into the relationship context, ongoing disputes, or scheduling issues the office team knows about.
  • No fabricated figures. Every balance, every aging calculation, every margin figure comes from deterministic, tested code. The language model explains the result in plain language; it never generates or adjusts a number.
  • Fully reversible. If an outreach action logs back to ServiceTitan incorrectly, or an escalation is misapplied, the prior state can be restored. The agent does not make irreversible writes.
  • Dispute-aware. The agent reads associated callback tasks and dispute notes from ServiceTitan. An invoice with an unresolved dispute never enters the standard chase queue; it goes to a separate review list.

Frequently asked questions

Does ServiceTitan have an AR collections tool built in?
ServiceTitan includes an accounts receivable module with aging reports, bulk statement printing, and online payment links. What it does not do is automatically prioritize which invoices to contact first, reconcile open balances against QuickBooks in real time, or propose the specific outreach for each invoice. Those steps require a human or an AI agent working on top of the native data.
Can AI send collection messages automatically, or does a human approve everything?
In Top Builder AI, no outreach goes to a customer without human approval. The Financial agent proposes the contact type, the message or call prompt, and the reason for each invoice, and an office manager approves, edits, or skips each one before anything is sent. Automated outreach without a human checkpoint creates relationship risk and ignores context the data cannot capture.
What is the difference between ServiceTitan AR and QuickBooks AR?
ServiceTitan's AR reflects the invoice status inside the field-service platform: billed, partially paid, open. QuickBooks AR reflects what the general ledger has actually received and reconciled. The two diverge when a payment is deposited in QuickBooks but not applied to the specific invoice in ServiceTitan, when credits or adjustments are entered in one system but not the other, or when the batch sync lags behind real-time transactions.
How does an AI agent prioritize which invoices to collect first?
The prioritization model weighs four factors together: age in days, invoice balance, the customer's payment history, and job margin. A large commercial invoice at 65 days from a customer with a known payment pattern is scored differently than a smaller residential invoice at the same age. The output is a ranked contact queue, not a raw aging report, with a recommended first-touch action per invoice.
What is a reconciliation mismatch and why does it matter for collections?
A reconciliation mismatch is an invoice that ServiceTitan shows as open but QuickBooks shows as partially or fully settled, usually because a payment was deposited but not applied to the specific invoice in the field-service platform. Chasing a mismatch means calling a customer who already paid, which damages the relationship. The Financial agent removes mismatches from the chase queue and surfaces them as a bookkeeping cleanup task instead.
Does the agent work for both commercial and residential invoices?
Yes, with different recommended contact methods. Residential customers under 30 days typically respond well to a payment-link SMS. Commercial accounts generally respond better to direct calls with a specific invoice reference. The agent's recommendation reflects the invoice type and the customer's payment history, and the office manager can override the recommendation before approving any outreach.
Will an AI collections agent contact customers without telling me?
No. Top Builder AI's Financial agent is advisory. It proposes the contact but takes no action until a human approves it. This matters because the agent does not know about ongoing callbacks, disputes, or relationship context the office team holds. The approval step is where that context applies before anything reaches the customer.
How does the agent handle disputed invoices?
The agent flags invoices that have an associated callback task or open dispute note in ServiceTitan and excludes them from the standard chase queue. They appear in a separate review list so the office team can resolve the underlying issue before any collections outreach. An unresolved dispute that gets chased as a normal invoice is one of the most common causes of a collectible invoice turning into a written-off one.
What does self-learning mean for the collections agent?
The Financial agent records the outcome of each approved outreach action, specifically whether the invoice was paid, partially paid, or required further follow-up after each contact type. Over time, it adjusts its recommendations per customer segment, for example learning that a specific commercial account category consistently pays within five business days of a call but rarely responds to SMS. The figures it reports are always computed by deterministic tested code, not by the AI model.
Can I connect Top Builder AI's Financial agent to my existing ServiceTitan and QuickBooks setup?
Yes. The Financial agent connects to both ServiceTitan and QuickBooks Online using your existing credentials. It reads the open-invoice report from ServiceTitan and the accounts receivable balance from QuickBooks to perform the reconciliation. No Zapier, no manual exports. The integration uses the same architecture as the other seven Top Builder AI agents already running on live ServiceTitan and QuickBooks installs.
How long before I see a reduction in outstanding AR?
The first cycle produces value immediately by surfacing any reconciliation mismatches that inflate the apparent aging, and by ranking the genuine open invoices so the day's follow-up effort goes to the highest-priority items first. How quickly the total outstanding balance falls depends on invoice volume, customer mix, and how consistently the office acts on the daily contact queue. Most shops see measurable movement in the 60-plus bucket within the first two to three weeks of consistent use.
Does the agent write to ServiceTitan or QuickBooks directly?
It reads from both systems. For outreach actions, all logging of approved contacts goes back into ServiceTitan as activity notes. The agent does not write payment records to QuickBooks directly; that is the bookkeeper's step after money clears. The distinction matters for audit integrity: the agent proposes and logs; humans and the accounting system confirm and record.
What is a typical DSO for an HVAC or plumbing shop on ServiceTitan?
Days Sales Outstanding varies by customer mix. A predominantly residential shop with online payment links typically collects within 10 to 20 days. A mixed residential and commercial shop often runs 30 to 45 days. A shop doing significant commercial work with net-30 or net-60 terms can easily sit at 50 to 70 days DSO as a structural baseline. The collections agent is most impactful for shops where the outstanding balance includes invoices well beyond their stated terms.
What happens to invoices that are too old to collect through normal follow-up?
The agent escalates invoices above a configurable age and balance threshold to a separate review queue and recommends a formal demand approach, such as a certified demand letter or referral to a collections agency. The specific threshold is set per shop. The agent documents the escalation and the prior contact history so whoever handles it has the full record without reconstructing it from ServiceTitan notes manually.
How does automating collections connect to the broader back-office picture?
The Financial agent's collections data feeds the cash-position report alongside cleared QuickBooks transactions and outstanding payables. The combined view lets an owner or fractional CFO answer the cash forecast question, specifically what cash will clear this week and next, rather than relying on billed revenue as a proxy. That is the difference between a board-ready financial summary and an accounting-only closing statement.
