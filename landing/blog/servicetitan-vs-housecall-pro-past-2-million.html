6 ServiceTitan vs Housecall Pro Differences That Matter Once a Contractor Bills Past $2M

Top Builder AI Published August 25, 2026 Updated August 25, 2026 ~12 min read Software Comparison

Housecall Pro publishes three prices. ServiceTitan publishes none. That one asymmetry runs through almost every real difference between them once a shop is big enough to seriously weigh both, past roughly $2M a year, multiple crews, and an office person doing more than invoicing at the kitchen table. Housecall Pro's own pricing page lists $59, $149, and $299/mo tiers by name. ServiceTitan's own pricing page shows three tiers, Starter, Essentials, and The Works, each behind a "Request Pricing" button. Below are six specific, sourced differences, not a marketing comparison, that actually change what a growing shop signs up for.

This isn't an argument that one platform beats the other. ServiceTitan is built for a scale of dispatch, reporting, and marketing tooling that a genuinely growing shop may need, and Housecall Pro is a real, capable platform for a huge share of home-service contractors who never need that much machinery. This blog already has a separate, narrower comparison of Jobber and Housecall Pro on job costing specifically. This one is about the six things that change once ServiceTitan is genuinely on the table.

Published Pricing

Housecall Pro names a number for every tier; ServiceTitan names none. Per Housecall Pro's own pricing page, Basic runs $59/mo (annual billing, 1 user included), Essentials runs $149/mo (5 users, additional users $100/mo each), and MAX runs $299/mo (8 users, additional users $75/mo each). ServiceTitan's own pricing page, by contrast, states "our per-technician pricing is designed to fit your business and goals, at any size" without ever stating what that pricing is, a structure confirmed independently on both Capterra (starting price listed as "Not available") and G2.

Real dollar figures for ServiceTitan do exist, just not on a rate card: ServiceTitan's own BBB complaints profile contains a filed complaint citing a $2,303 monthly bill (Tiger Air), a separate complaint citing $1,400/mo "across all contacts and locations," and a third citing $120,000/yr (Bold City). None specify technician count, so none convert into a clean per-tech rate, but they are real, dated, named figures.

Contract Length and Cancellation

Housecall Pro's own pricing page states this in plain language: "unlike some other field service management software, Housecall Pro does not require long-term contracts. Cancelling is simple and hassle-free." The same page describes month-to-month or annual billing with the ability to "downgrade anytime with changes taking effect at the end of your current monthly or annual billing period."

ServiceTitan's default under its own Terms of Use is also month-to-month when no separate agreement exists, but that default rarely governs what a shop actually signs. The same Terms of Use state plainly: "If you want to terminate the Agreement before the term of the Agreement is over, you will owe a termination fee equal to all the remaining payments." Real signed contracts visible through ServiceTitan's BBB complaints profile run 24 months, with termination demands from $16,121 (Tiger Air, 7 remaining months) to $67,230 (Quality Comfort). A Software Advice reviewer, writing independently, described owing "the remaining roughly 22k balance to get out."

Job Costing Depth

Housecall Pro names more cost categories, at a lower price, in its own documentation. Per Housecall Pro's own Job Costing set-up guide, it tracks labor at a "fully loaded rate" (base pay plus employer-paid taxes, insurance, and benefits, multiplied by time on the job including travel), materials, miscellaneous expenses, commissions, and service line-item unit costs, with the feature named on the $59/mo Basic tier itself.

ServiceTitan markets enterprise-grade job costing and reporting, but because pricing and feature depth sit behind a sales quote rather than a public page, that depth cannot be checked the same way Housecall Pro's can, against a documented feature list anyone can open. That opacity is the same pattern this blog has documented on ServiceTitan's pricing and contract terms specifically.

QuickBooks Online Sync

Housecall Pro documents exactly what moves, and it isn't job cost. Per Housecall Pro's own help article on syncing to QuickBooks Online, the integration moves invoices, payments, new customer records, and new price book items, one-way, into QuickBooks. Job costing and labor cost don't appear anywhere on that list, and estimates never transfer at all. QuickBooks Online sync itself first appears on the $149/mo Essentials tier, per Housecall Pro's own pricing page, not on the $59/mo Basic plan.

ServiceTitan's own site does not publish the same level of sync detail publicly, which is consistent with its broader pattern of routing specifics to a sales conversation rather than a documentation page. A shop evaluating either platform for the QuickBooks question specifically should ask for that sync scope in writing before assuming it covers job cost.

Onboarding: Vendor-Led vs Self-Serve

The two platforms hand a growing shop two different jobs. ServiceTitan's own onboarding page describes a vendor-led process that "ensures your entire team is efficiently trained," with no fee or timeline disclosed on the page itself. The one confirmed figure comes from a filed BBB complaint: Quality Comfort paid $4,802.50 via ACH for onboarding and initial fees, part of a 24-month, $96,000 contract.

Housecall Pro discloses no separate onboarding fee anywhere on its pricing page. Its own Job Costing set-up guide instead describes a self-serve, multi-step process a shop's own staff completes: enabling the Job Costing Breakdown feature, setting up time tracking, configuring fully loaded employee rates, building out a Price Book, and enabling material and miscellaneous job inputs. Neither vendor's own documentation names a specific timeline for either process, but one is a paid, vendor-run implementation with a documented dollar figure; the other is a self-configured setup with none.

Add-On Structure and Total Stack Cost

ServiceTitan layers named, unpriced add-ons on top of an unpriced base subscription. ServiceTitan's own Pricebook Pro feature page lists Pricebook Pro under a "Get more with Pro" upgrade section alongside Marketing Pro, Fleet Pro, and the AI Virtual Agent, with no price shown for any of them. Capterra reviewers separately cite "costly add-ons, undisclosed fees, and a pricing structure that challenges smaller businesses" as a specific criticism, distinct from praise for the platform's core feature depth.

Housecall Pro's stack cost, by contrast, is entirely readable from one page: a shop knows at signup that moving from Basic to Essentials to MAX costs $59, $149, or $299/mo, plus a published per-seat add-on rate ($100/mo on Essentials, $75/mo on MAX) once the included user count is exceeded. There's no equivalent public number for what a ServiceTitan Pro add-on adds to the base bill.

How we chose these six

We started from what actually changes a signing decision once a shop is weighing both platforms seriously, not every feature either vendor lists. Pricing, contract terms, and onboarding cost were chosen because they're what a shop signs, not what it uses day to day; job costing and QuickBooks sync were chosen because they're the two features this blog's audience asks about most; add-on structure was chosen because it's where ServiceTitan's total cost most often diverges from the sales quote a shop expected. Every figure traces to that vendor's own current pricing page, Terms of Use, feature page, or a filed BBB complaint, opened directly, not a review site's paraphrase of it.

What should actually decide this for a shop past $2M?

Whether the six differences above matter more than the dispatch, reporting, and marketing depth ServiceTitan is built for, which this piece doesn't score, that's a fit question specific to a shop's own crew size and complexity. What's genuinely confirmed either way: Housecall Pro's total cost and contract exposure are readable from one page before a sales call happens; ServiceTitan's are not, and the real dollar figures that do exist come from BBB complaints and signed contracts, not a rate card.

What else do contractors ask about ServiceTitan vs Housecall Pro?

What is the biggest difference between ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro?
Whether you can get a price without a sales call. Housecall Pro's own pricing page lists three tiers with real monthly numbers, $59, $149, and $299. ServiceTitan's own pricing page shows three tiers, Starter, Essentials, and The Works, each with a Request Pricing button and no dollar figure anywhere on the page, confirmed the same way on Capterra and G2.
Does ServiceTitan require a long-term contract?
In practice, yes, even though ServiceTitan's own Terms of Use default to month-to-month when no separate order form exists. Real signed contracts visible through ServiceTitan's own BBB complaints profile run 24 months, and the same Terms of Use state a shop that cancels early owes "a termination fee equal to all the remaining payments."
Does Housecall Pro require a long-term contract?
No. Housecall Pro's own pricing page states directly: "unlike some other field service management software, Housecall Pro does not require long-term contracts. Cancelling is simple and hassle-free," with month-to-month or annual billing and the ability to downgrade at the end of the current billing period.
How much does ServiceTitan actually cost, since it won't publish a price?
No public number exists, but real dollar figures do, from ServiceTitan's own BBB complaints profile: a $2,303 monthly bill (Tiger Air), a $1,400 monthly bill (a separate 2025 complaint), and a $120,000 annual bill (Bold City). None specify technician count, so they cannot be converted into a clean per-tech rate.
How much does Housecall Pro cost per month?
Per Housecall Pro's own pricing page: Basic is $59/mo (annual billing, 1 user), Essentials is $149/mo (5 users, additional users $100/mo each), and MAX is $299/mo (8 users, additional users $75/mo each).
Which platform has deeper job costing?
Housecall Pro names more cost categories at a lower price: labor at a fully loaded rate including travel time, materials, miscellaneous expenses, commissions, and service line items, per Housecall Pro's own Job Costing set-up guide, available starting on the $59/mo Basic plan. ServiceTitan's job costing is positioned as enterprise-grade but sits behind a sales quote, so its depth cannot be verified against a public feature list the way Housecall Pro's can.
Does either platform's QuickBooks sync carry job cost into the books?
Housecall Pro's does not. Per Housecall Pro's own help article on syncing to QuickBooks Online, the integration moves invoices, payments, new customer records, and new price book items, one-way, with job costing and labor cost absent from that list. ServiceTitan's own site does not publish the technical scope of its QuickBooks sync in the same level of detail.
How much does ServiceTitan's onboarding cost?
ServiceTitan's own onboarding page describes the process but discloses no fee. The one confirmed figure comes from a filed BBB complaint: Quality Comfort paid $4,802.50 via ACH for onboarding and initial fees on a 24-month, $96,000 contract.
Does Housecall Pro charge an onboarding fee?
Nothing on Housecall Pro's own pricing page or its Job Costing set-up guide names a separate onboarding fee. The set-up guide describes a self-serve, multi-step configuration process, enabling the Job Costing Breakdown feature, setting fully loaded employee rates, and building a Price Book, that a shop's own staff completes rather than a vendor-led implementation.
Do ServiceTitan's Pro add-ons cost extra on top of the base subscription?
Yes. ServiceTitan's own Pricebook Pro feature page lists it under a "Get more with Pro" upgrade section alongside Marketing Pro, Fleet Pro, and the AI Virtual Agent, with no price shown for any of them. Capterra reviewers separately cite "costly add-ons, undisclosed fees, and a pricing structure that challenges smaller businesses" as a specific criticism.
Is this saying Housecall Pro is better than ServiceTitan?
No. ServiceTitan is built for a different scale of operation, deeper dispatch, reporting, and marketing tooling that a shop genuinely outgrowing Housecall Pro may need. This piece is narrower: six specific, sourced differences worth knowing before that decision, not a verdict on which platform wins.
