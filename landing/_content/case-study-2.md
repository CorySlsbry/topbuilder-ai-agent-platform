# How a 14-tech HVAC shop clawed back 38 days of cash and 8 office hours a week without hiring

_Representative 14-technician residential and light-commercial HVAC contractor (~$6M annual revenue, one office manager running the back office)_

> DISCLAIMER: This is an illustrative benchmark scenario, not a specific client. Top Builder AI is pre-launch, so no real customer results exist yet. Every figure below is modeled from published 2026 industry benchmarks (HVAC days-sales-outstanding ranges, small-business late-payment and bad-debt data, and administrative-time studies) applied to a representative shop, not measured from one company's books. Your numbers will differ based on your revenue, billing mix, and current process.

## Challenge
A growing HVAC shop ran ServiceTitan in the field and QuickBooks in the office, with one office manager keying everything in between. She built invoices by hand from completed jobs, chased payments by phone when she had time, filed permits and warranty paperwork into folders nobody could find, and answered the phones. The cracks were predictable. Invoices went out days after the job closed. Accounts receivable aged to roughly 52 days, the upper end of the HVAC average, while best-run shops collect inside 35. Roughly 1.8% of revenue quietly turned into bad debt because nobody had time to work the 60-and-90-day column. Paperwork piled up. The owner's real choice looked like hiring a second admin at $50,000 to $60,000 fully loaded, or watching collections and compliance slip further. Neither fixed the root problem: the office was doing by hand what the field systems already knew.

## Approach
Top Builder AI installed on top of the existing ServiceTitan and QuickBooks stack. It did not replace either one. The Integration Bridge read completed-job data from ServiceTitan, and three back-office agents went to work. The Documents agent classified and filed every permit, warranty, and signed work order automatically, and flagged anything missing a signature. The Booking agent answered and triaged after-hours calls so jobs got on the board instead of going to voicemail. The Collections agent watched A/R aging in real time and drafted prioritized, dollar-accurate follow-ups the moment an invoice crossed each threshold. Every dollar and every aging day was computed by tested code, not guessed by the AI, so the office manager could trust the numbers and the audit trail. The agents ran Approve-first at the start: she reviewed each draft for a few weeks, then moved the routine collections reminders to Auto once she trusted them, keeping full undo and a logged history. She went from keying data to approving decisions.

## Results (modeled)
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO): 52 days down to 34 days** — Modeled from the published HVAC DSO range (35-55 days average, best-in-class under 35; ClearReceivables and Construction Cost Accounting 2026). Earlier, dollar-accurate aging follow-up from the Collections agent moves a high-average shop toward the best-in-class end.
- **One-time working capital freed: about $296,000** — On ~$6M revenue, one DSO day ties up roughly $16,400 of cash ($6M / 365). Reclaiming 18 DSO days frees a one-time ~$296K of cash already earned but uncollected. This is cash pulled forward, not new revenue.
- **Bad debt recovered annually: about $54,000 per year** — Modeled at 1.8% of $6M revenue (~$108K) written off because aged invoices were never worked, conservative against data showing 32% of businesses lose 5-30% of revenue to bad debt and outstanding invoices average ~11% of revenue (Old National Bank / PYMNTS 2025). Systematic threshold-based follow-up is modeled to recover roughly half.
- **Office-manager hours reclaimed: about 8 hours per week** — Administrative-automation studies show 6-10 hours/week saved per employee on invoicing, follow-up, and data entry, with admins spending ~44% of the week on invoice creation and ~43% on data entry (Time etc; business.com 2026). Modeled at the midpoint for invoicing, collections drafting, and document filing combined.
- **Avoided back-office hire: $50,000 to $60,000 per year not spent** — Reclaiming ~8 hours/week (~400 hours/year) absorbs the growth that would otherwise require a second admin. Figure is the fully loaded cost of one additional office/admin role at 2026 small-business pay rates.
