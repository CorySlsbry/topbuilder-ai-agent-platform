The Margin Cheat Sheet

1Labor burden

  • True hourly cost = wage x burden. Typical range for contractors we work with: 1.25x to 1.4x.
  • Compute once a year: employer taxes + comp + insurance + benefits + truck + fuel + phone + tools, divided by productive field hours.
  • Cost every job at the loaded rate, never the paystub wage.

2Overhead allocation

  • Overhead rate = last year's overhead / last year's revenue.
  • Every job carries that % of its revenue. Or divide overhead by field hours and load it per hour.
  • Pick one method, stay consistent, review the rate twice a year.

3Verdict bands (net after overhead)

  • 10%+ = healthy. Save the estimate as a template and hold the price.
  • 0-10% = thin. Find the biggest bucket, then fix price or hours.
  • Below 0% = underwater. Stop selling it at this price. Reprice or re-scope first.

4The weekly 20-minute review

  • Pull last week's closed jobs. Load burdened labor, materials, equipment, subs.
  • Post callbacks to the job that caused them. Subtract each job's overhead share.
  • Flag anything under target and ask why: estimate, execution, or price.

5A flagged job is one of three problems

  • Estimate wrong: fix the template so the next bid is right.
  • Execution slipped: hours, waste, rework. Coach the crew.
  • Price too low: raise it or walk away.

6Chronically thin job type? Four moves

  • Reprice it against the true cost.
  • Re-scope what is included by default.
  • Retrain the crew that runs long.
  • Retire it and sell the profitable work instead.
